Not Enough Pins for Your Keyboard Matrix?

SPI Shift Registers 74HC595 · 74HC165 · 74HC589 Matrix Remapping (5×13 → 8×9) QMK & ZMK

So you decided to wire a custom mechanical keyboard, but your favorite compact microcontroller doesn't expose enough GPIO pins for the full switch matrix. This is the exact challenge I hit when moving the 65-key m65 onto the tiny 11-pin Seeed Studio XIAO RP2040.

Common Pin-Reduction Approaches

Before settling on SPI shift registers, I surveyed the existing approaches used in the community:

  • Duplex Matrix: Folds long rows/columns by wiring two switches per row/column intersection with opposing diodes. Effective, but makes schematic reading and routing more mentally taxing.
  • Direct-Pin Shift Registers: Used in boards like tzarc's Ghoul with chained 74HC589 shift registers (one bit per switch, no matrix). Great scan speed, but requires many ICs for 65+ keys.
  • I²C IO Expanders & Demuxers: Popular on split boards (e.g., Ferris IO Expander guide) or 74HC138 3-to-8 decoders in QMK.
  • SPI Shift-Register Matrix Scanning: Using inexpensive 74HC595 (serial-in, parallel-out) and 74HC165 / 74HC589 (parallel-in, serial-out) shift registers to drive columns and/or read rows over hardware SPI.
    • Background tutorials on shift registers: Last Minute Engineers — 74HC595 and DroneBot Workshop — 74HC595 & 74HC165.
    • Initial keyboard inspiration: mehmedbasic.dk — 74HC595 Keyboard.

Official QMK Support & Acknowledgments

When I started experimenting with these circuits, QMK had very few public examples. Since QMK `0.20.0`, boards like Keychron and `hazel/bad_wings` use `74HC595`, and `handwired/dqz11n1g` uses `74HC165`. Huge thanks to **sigprof** and **tzarc** for their guidance on SPI timing and tri-state buffering.

Disclaimer

While the schematics and firmware below work reliably together to scan a 13×5 / 14×5 matrix at 4,000–6,000+ Hz, remember I am a hardware enthusiast rather than an electrical engineer — use and adapt them at your own risk!

Method 1: Scan Columns via 74HC595 + Direct GPIO Rows

Uses 2× 74HC595 output shift registers chained over SPI (MOSI, SCK, RCLK) to drive up to 16 columns using only 3 MCU pins, while the 5 rows are read directly on 5 MCU GPIO pins (total: 8 pins for a 5×16 matrix).

  • QMK Implementation: mlego/m65/rev7/matrix.c
  • ZMK Implementation: mlego5x13_rev8.overlay (zmk,kscan-gpio-matrix + 74hc595)

Method 2a: Scan Columns (74HC595) & Rows (74HC165) — Unbuffered

Uses 2× 74HC595 (columns) and 1× 74HC165 (rows). Both ICs are inexpensive and widely available in through-hole (DIP-16) and SMD (SOIC-16) packages. Reads the entire 5×14 matrix using only 4 MCU pins (MOSI, MISO, SCK, shared Latch).

Limitation: Unbuffered MISO

Because the `74HC165` serial output (`Q7`) does not have a true high-impedance (3-state) output when deselected, **you cannot easily share the `MISO` pin with other SPI peripherals** without adding external tri-state buffer circuitry.

  • QMK Implementation: mlego/m66/rev1/matrix.c

Method 2b: Scan Columns (74HC595) & Rows (74HC589) — Buffered 3-State SPI

Replaces the 74HC165 with a 1× 74HC589 (mostly SMD today). The 74HC589 includes an internal 3-state output buffer, allowing the MISO line to be cleanly shared with other SPI devices (such as SPI e-ink or Sharp Memory displays).

Variant 1 (Single Shared Latch — Deprecated)

Do Not Use Variant 1 for New Designs

While this single-latch circuit (`m65 Rev 10` / `m66 Rev 2`) works with carefully tuned firmware timing, it relies on edge-case latch/output-enable behavior. Use **Variant 2** below instead.

  • QMK Reference: mlego/m66/rev2/matrix.c

Variant 2 (Separate Chip Selects — Recommended)

Uses separate latch / chip-select lines for the 2× 74HC595 column driver and the 1× 74HC589 row reader (costing 1 extra pin compared to Variant 1, for 5 SPI pins total), making SPI bus sharing and matrix scanning clean and deterministic.

  • QMK Implementation: mlego/m66/rev3/matrix.c

Hybrid Scanning

All three shift-register methods above can be combined with direct GPIO pins in custom `matrix.c` routines if you want to mix shift-register columns with extra direct keys.

Method 3: Mathematical Matrix Restructuring (Zero Extra Hardware)

If you are only short by 1 or 2 GPIO pins, you don't need any shift registers at all — simply decouple the electrical matrix dimensions from the physical key layout so the row and column counts are closer to a square (Rows ≈ Cols maximizes Rows × Cols for a fixed pin budget Rows + Cols).

  • Example 1: A physical 6×17 board (68 keys) normally requires 6 + 17 = 23 pins. Electrically wiring it as a 10×7 or 9×8 matrix reduces the pin count to 17 pins — saving 6 pins!
  • Example 2 (m65 5×13 Ortho):
    • Standard 5×13 Matrix: Uses 5 + 13 = 18 pins (65 positions).
    • Restructured 8×9 Matrix: Uses 8 + 9 = 17 pins (72 positions, saving 1 pin with 7 spare switch slots!).

Both QMK (via data-driven info.json "matrix": [row, col] coordinates) and ZMK (via zmk,matrix-transform) make this remapping trivial:
