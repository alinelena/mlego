m65 (5×13 Ortho)

5×13 Ortho 65 Keys STM32F401 / RP2040 / XIAO QMK & ZMK
All Keyboards

m65 — 5×13 LEGO Ortholinear Keyboard

Status: Rev 11 Tested 5×13 Ortho (65–66 Keys) XIAO RP2040 / BLE · Pico · STM32 SPI Shift Registers (74HC595 / 589 / 165) OLED & E-Ink + Rotary Encoder QMK & ZMK

The m65 is a 5-row, 13-column ortholinear mechanical keyboard designed to accommodate all standard keycaps from an ISO layout.

In its later iterations (Rev 7–Rev 11), it departs from a traditional direct-pin matrix and uses the tiny Seeed Studio XIAO RP2040 or Seeed Studio XIAO nRF52840 BLE (SAMD21 may also work). Because the XIAO only exposes 11 GPIO pins, rows are scanned via a 74HC589 (or 74HC165) input shift register while columns are driven by two chained 74HC595 output shift registers over hardware SPI (sharing clock and latch pins), leaving pins free for an I²C OLED / SPI e-ink screen and a clickable rotary encoder (making Rev 9–11 technically a 14-column matrix, later spun off into the m66 line).

Seeed Studio Sponsorship

For **Rev 7**, Seeed Studio kindly sponsored the 5×13 version with PCBs and XIAO microcontrollers as part of the [Seeed Fusion DIY XIAO Mechanical Keyboard Contest](https://www.seeedstudio.com/seeed-fusion-diy-xiao-mechanical-keyboard-contest.html) and their [Fusion PCB Assembly service](https://www.seeedstudio.com/pcb-assembly.html).

Revision History 

* **Rev 11**: XIAO RP2040 with 3 shift registers (2× `74HC595` + 1× `74HC589`), OLED / e-ink (`SSD1680` WeAct), and push rotary encoder with QMK (actively exploring ZMK support for RP2040 and nRF52840)
* **Rev 10**: XIAO RP2040 with 3 shift registers (2× `74HC595` + 1× `74HC589`), OLED, and push rotary encoder with QMK
* **Rev 9**: XIAO RP2040 with 3 shift registers (2× `74HC595` + 1× `74HC165`), OLED, and push rotary encoder with QMK
* **Rev 7/8**: XIAO RP2040 and nRF52840 with 2× `74HC595` shift registers, rotary encoder, no OLED (both QMK and ZMK firmware supported)
* **Rev 5/6**: `RP2040` support (Raspberry Pi Pico, WeAct RP2040, and compatible clones), non-push encoder, OLED
* **Rev 4**: `STM32F401`/`F411` BlackPill, non-push encoder, OLED
* **Rev 3**: `STM32F401`/`F411` BlackPill, non-push encoder
* **Rev 2**: `GD32F303` aka [BluePill Plus](https://github.com/WeActStudio/BluePill-Plus) (USB-C `F103` equivalent)
* **Rev 1**: `STM32F103` / `APM32F103` or `STM32F303`

Microcontrollers

MCU Selection Guide 

Using off-the-shelf development boards makes DIY keyboard design much simpler because all high-frequency crystal and USB protection circuitry lives on the MCU daughterboard. While I have built handwired boards with AVR (`ATmega32U4`), I strongly advise against using AVR for new designs.

* **Rev 1**: [`STM32F103C8T6` BluePill](https://stm32-base.org/boards/STM32F103C8T6-Blue-Pill.html) or [RobotDyn `STM32F303CCT6` BlackPill](https://stm32-base.org/boards/STM32F303CCT6-RobotDyn-Black-Pill.html) *(do not confuse with the WeAct F401/F411 BlackPill)*. Micro-USB; requires flashing an `stm32duino` bootloader.
* **Rev 2**: `GD32F303` [BluePill Plus](https://github.com/WeActStudio/BluePill-Plus) with USB Type-C.
* **Rev 3 / Rev 4**: WeAct [`STM32F401`/`F411` MiniSTM32F4x BlackPill](https://github.com/WeActStudio/WeActStudio.MiniSTM32F4x) with USB Type-C.
* **Rev 5 / Rev 6**: `RP2040` ([Raspberry Pi Pico](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/microcontrollers/raspberry-pi-pico.html), [Waveshare RP2040-Plus](https://www.waveshare.com/wiki/RP2040-Plus), or USB-C compatible clones — verify pinout compatibility).
* **Rev 7 / Rev 9 / Rev 10 / Rev 11**: [Seeed Studio XIAO RP2040](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/XIAO-RP2040/) and [Seeed Studio XIAO BLE nRF52840](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/XIAO_BLE/). Ultra-compact footprint using SPI shift registers for matrix scanning.
* **Need more pins?**: Consider the [WeAct `STM32G431` CoreBoard](https://github.com/WeActStudio/WeActStudio.STM32G431CoreBoard), already supported in QMK.

Component Lists by Revision

Rev 11

Revision 11 BOM 

* **LEGO Case**: ~46× 1×2 or 1×4 plates (use full bricks if housing a battery), 1× 16×32 double-sided baseplate or 2× 16×16 plates (gray compatible plates minimize PCB flexing), optional 4× 1×1 tiles/eyes and 4× 2×2 corner plates

* **Shift Registers**: 2× `74HC595` (SOIC-16 SMD) and 1× `74HC589` (SOIC-16 SMD)
* **MOSFET**: 1× `2N7002` FET (SOT-23 SMD)
* **Capacitors (optional)**: 3× 100 nF decoupling capacitors (THT, SMD, or integrated socket)
* **Battery / Wireless (optional)**: Pogo pins, LiPo battery, 2-pin PH 2.0 battery socket, and 1P2T 4 mm micro slide switch
* **Encoder**: Clickable EC11 rotary encoder (Bourns 24-pulse incremental encoder with 6 mm flat shaft)
* **Displays (optional)**:
    * 128×32 I²C OLED (4-pin) + 1×4 DIL socket
    * WeAct SSD1680 e-ink module + 2×4 header and socket

Revision 5/6 BOM

Revision 4 BOM (and Rev 1–3) 

* **LEGO Case**: 16×32 double-sided plate + ~46× 1×2 or 1×4 plates
* **Diodes**: 65× `1N4148` signal diodes (DO-35 or SOD-123)

Thickness Profiles

Depending on how many intermediate LEGO layers you stack, the enclosure can be built ultra-slim or deep enough for JST headers and acoustic foam:

  • 8.0 mm — Slim Profile

    1× baseplate (single or double-sided, flat or angled) + 1× intermediate plate (3.2 mm) + PCB (1.6 mm). Leaves room for a thin sheet of sound-dampening foam.

  • 11.2 mm — Dual-Plate Profile

    1× double-sided baseplate + 2× intermediate plates + PCB. Fits thicker foam or a hand-soldered LED strip (without a JST connector).

  • 14.4 mm — Full Brick Profile

    1× double-sided baseplate + 1× full LEGO brick + PCB. Provides clearance for a 3-pin JST header or LiPo battery.

  3. Rev 9: Includes an extra 14th column compared to earlier revisions; SPI_DIVISOR is 8 on Rev 9 vs 32 on Rev 7.

Impact of OLED_UPDATE_INTERVAL

Updating an I²C OLED display on every matrix scan causes a noticeable drop in scan rate. QMK provides OLED_UPDATE_INTERVAL (in milliseconds) to throttle display refreshes. Tested on Rev 9 (128×32 and 128×64 screens yield identical numbers; baselines with OLED off: 3760 Hz with WPM off, 2631 Hz with WPM on):

OLED_UPDATE_INTERVAL [ms] Matrix Scan Frequency [Hz]
0 (QMK default) 2125
1 3154
2 3629
3 3788
4 3868
5 3915
10 (100 FPS — recommended) 4011
20 4058
30 4075
50 4087
100 4097

Takeaways

1. Setting `OLED_UPDATE_INTERVAL` to **`10` ms** (100 FPS) nearly doubles the effective matrix scan rate (`4011 Hz` vs `2125 Hz`) while remaining more than fast enough for any keyboard status OLED.
2. Curiously, throttling the OLED refresh achieves a higher peak scan rate (`4097 Hz`) than compiling with the OLED disabled (`3760 Hz`).
